Who Built Sley?

Sley is developed by Greyforge Labs. The correct public entity chain is Sley, sleylang.org, Greyforge Labs, and greyforge.tech.

Why Build A New Language?

Because agent-written software exposes a control-surface problem. Most languages force agents to work through raw text and then hope compiler errors, tests, and human review catch the damage afterward.

Sley starts from a different premise: the language and compiler should make structure, authority, diagnostics, planned edits, verification, and evidence first-class surfaces.

Is Sley Open Source?

Yes. Sley is publicly released as v1.0 under the Apache-2.0 license at github.com/GreyforgeLabs/sley.

Why Not Publish The Spec Now?

Because some internal implementation details are still being stabilized for v1.x hardening. Public artifacts are intentionally scoped to reduce operational risk while keeping the language and roadmap visible.

Is Sley Production Ready?

Yes, Sley v1.0 is publicly released. It is suitable for open evaluation and controlled production trials, with explicit environment and integration review for sensitive workflows.

Does Sley Call Live External Systems?

Publicly, sensitive host paths are represented through explicit, deterministic gates and policy checks. Live provider calls, shell execution, secret-store reads, deployment actions, and spend behavior are only permitted through explicit operator policy.
