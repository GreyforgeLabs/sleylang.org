Current project status

The human-readable line is complete.

Greyforge has frozen active Sley 1.x feature development. The Apache-2.0 repository remains open for use, study, extension, experimentation, and forks. Sley 2.x is a clean machine-native architecture where human governance remains required but direct readability of the canonical raw representation is optional. Compatibility is not promised.

Why Sley

Move from inferred patches to explicit change.

Software agents are increasingly capable, but most language surfaces still make them reconstruct intent from raw text. Sley gives that work a structural path that people and tools can inspect together.

Text-only change

Intent lives around the code.

Scope, authority, risk, and proof must be repeatedly explained, inferred, and reconstructed across sessions.

→
Structural change with Sley

Intent travels with the work.

Inspection, planned edits, diagnostics, authority checks, and evidence become explicit parts of the change lifecycle.

Language surface

Designed around how software changes.

Sley is more than a syntax proposal. Its public capability model connects readable source to bounded tool surfaces and evidence-aware engineering workflows.

01

Structural inspection

Expose relevant program shape, relationships, and diagnostics without forcing every workflow to begin from full raw files.

02

Planned edits

Represent candidate changes as narrow, reviewable intentions before accepting source mutation.

03

Authority boundaries

Keep effectful or sensitive work behind explicit gates that can be evaluated before action.

04

Verification artifacts

Carry diagnostics, checks, provenance, traces, and handoff evidence alongside accepted work.

05

Read-only tool bridges

Support bounded inspection and planning surfaces for external development tools without granting write authority by default.

06

Semantic comparison

Report meaningful structural overlap and difference while keeping merge decisions under explicit review.

The change loop

A clearer contract between autonomy and review.

Each stage makes a different question answerable before the workflow moves forward.

  1. 01

    Inspect the structure

    What part of the system actually matters to this change?

  2. 02

    Plan the delta

    What is proposed, what stays untouched, and what could be affected?

  3. 03

    Check authority

    Is the requested effect within the declared and approved boundary?

  4. 04

    Verify the result

    What evidence proves the accepted change is ready for review or handoff?
