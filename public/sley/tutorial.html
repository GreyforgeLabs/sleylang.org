Sley / Walkthrough

The structural change journey

From intent to evidence.

This walkthrough follows Sley's product model through a realistic software change and shows what each structural stage contributes.

Scenario

Change validation behavior across a multi-module project.

The operator wants a narrow behavior change. Several modules depend on the affected surface. The workflow needs to protect unrelated code, respect authority boundaries, and leave enough evidence for a human reviewer.

Desired outcome

A scoped, reviewable change whose impact and verification record are explicit.

01

Frame the request

The operator states the intended behavior and the review boundary. Success becomes explicit, with scope centered on the source surfaces that serve the objective.

OUTPUT: clear intent and bounded objective
02

Inspect the relevant structure

The workflow asks for the program shape, relationships, and diagnostics connected to the target. A focused structural slice establishes the working context.

OUTPUT: structural slice and affected relationships
03

Draft a narrow plan

A candidate transformation describes the proposed delta, the expected impact, and the source surfaces that remain untouched. The plan is reviewable before a write is accepted.

OUTPUT: planned delta with explicit scope
04

Read diagnostics as constraints

Compiler signals provide structured information about correctness, effect, and risk. The workflow uses those signals to refine the plan.

OUTPUT: checked assumptions and visible decision points
05

Evaluate authority

For consequential effects, the workflow reports structural validity and execution authority as separate, explicit states. The operator retains clear control over action.

OUTPUT: declared authority state
06

Verify and hand off

The accepted path carries its diagnostics, checks, provenance, and handoff evidence forward. A human reviewer can see what changed, why it changed, and what proof supports acceptance.

OUTPUT: review projection and evidence-ready handoff

What improves

The workflow ends with explicit, reviewable intent.

Sley gives engineering judgment a clearer structural surface.

01

Review sees intent

The proposed behavior and its scope are visible alongside the resulting change.

02

Tools see structure

Relevant program shape is available as a focused compiler-exposed work surface.

03

Authority stays explicit

Capability and permission remain separate throughout the workflow.

04

Evidence survives handoff

Checks and provenance remain attached to the accepted path.

Review checklist

A Sley-shaped change should answer these questions.

  • Is the requested outcome clear?
  • Is the inspected context bounded to the work?
  • Is the candidate delta narrow and reviewable?
  • Are diagnostics and risk signals visible?
  • Is authority established before consequential action?
  • Does the handoff include verification evidence?
